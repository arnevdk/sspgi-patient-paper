\begin{letter}{Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation \\ Springer Nature group}

	\opening{Dear editors of the Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation,}

	We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled ``Visual ERP-based
	brain-computer interface use with severe physical, speech and eye movement
	impairments: case studies'' for consideration to publish in your journal.

	This study is situated at the intersection of neuroscience, biomedical
	engineering, and physical medicine and rehabilitation, and is therefore well
	aligned with the scope of JNER.
	It directly contributes to ongoing efforts to advance assistive technologies
	that support communication in individuals with severe motor and speech impairments,
	especially those with limited or no eye motor control.
	The work proposes, implements, and evaluates a brain-computer interface
	(BCI) with immediate clinical relevance for communication rehabilitation.

	The manuscript presents a series of case studies involving individuals with
	severe speech, physical, and gaze impairments (SSPGI), evaluating the
	feasibility and usability of a gaze-independent visual oddball BCI.
	We assessed performance across three visuospatial attention (VSA) conditions -- overt,
	covert,	and free VSA -- in participants with varying levels of gaze control.
	Our results	confirm that while covert attention with cued central fixation leads
	to reduced accuracy, free VSA, where gaze is unconstrained, often performs on
	par with overt VSA.
	Importantly, we demonstrate that decoder training under overt VSA
	conditions can improve subsequent decoding performance in covert or free
	settings.
	These findings suggest that residual eye motor control can be leveraged
	to improve BCI operation, even in individuals with severe impairments.

	This work addresses a major gap in the literature by focusing on realistic users with
	SSPGI, rather than healthy controls.
	It offers actionable insights into the adaptation of visual BCI protocols for
	user comfort and performance,	highlights the importance of user-centered
	design, and proposes recommendations for future clinical implementation.
	The study also provides a combination of detailed clinical,
	neurophysiological, and behavioral data in this	understudied population.

	We believe our findings will be of interest to your readership, particularly
	those working on novel BCI paradigms, clinical neurorehabilitation technologies,
	and translational research in assistive communication.

	Thank you for considering our manuscript for publication in Journal of
	NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation. We look forward to your response.


	\closing{Sincerely and on behalf of all authors,}
