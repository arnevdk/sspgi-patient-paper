Table 1:
  We have implemented the reviewer's helpful terminology remarks in Table 1. The
  Kübler and Birbaumer classification system is included here, as there is, to
  the authors' knowledge, no universally agreed-upon standard system within the
  field for classification of BCI user capabilities. Some readers may find the
  Wolpaw criteria clearer due to their simplicity. This system has now been
  explained clearly, as it is the main system used for recruitment in this study
  according to the predefined inclusion criterion. However, some readers may
  prefer the more granular and potentially more widely used classification
  proposed by Kübler and Birbaumer. Both systems were introduced around the same
  time (2006 and 2008). Hence, both systems are included, with Wolpaw's
  classification as the main one and the Kübler–Birbaumer classification as a
  reference for interested readers or for those more familiar with it. The table
  caption now clarifies this.

Section 2.2:
* We did indeed define a semi-rigid list of questions to assess participants'
  eye movement capabilities and vision. These questions are now included in
  Appendix C. Where possible, the assessments obtained from these questions were
  supplemented with details from the medical file or an oculomotor assessment
  provided by the clinician.

* The line between minor and major impairment in our earlier assessment of
  subjects was drawn rather subjectively. We have now reevaluated this using
  fixed rules: Major impairment implies that the impairment was complete, or
  that impairment of this ability alone prevents or hampers the participant from
  operating a regular visual oddball BCI as a healthy control subject would,
  after potential visual corrections. Minor impairment implies that the ability
  is affected or causes discomfort, but does not prevent the user from operating
  a visual BCI as a healthy control subject would, after potential visual
  corrections. These rules have now been included in Section 2.2 in the revised
  manuscript on lines 178-182. Following these rules, involuntary movements of
  participants B1 and C3 were reclassified from minor to major.

* The structured questionnaire used for our assessment included age,
  handedness, sex, exact neuromuscular diagnosis, time of neuromuscular
  diagnosis, stage of neuromuscular disease if applicable, and ophthalmological
  diagnosis. These questions were asked by the experimenter(s) and answered by
  the participant or the paramedical team. Other relevant clinical details were
  obtained from the clinician where possible. Further details concerning
  communication, pragmatics, and presentation of the participant were observed
  by the experimenter(s) or gathered through conversation with the participant
  or the paramedical team. This is now clearly stated at the end of Section 2.1
  in the revised manuscript and Appendix B has been added to provide the
  questionnaire.

* All participants using corrective lenses wore them during the assessment of
  eye movements and visual acuity, and throughout the BCI session. This is
  clarified in the revised manuscript on line 176.

* Initially, Figure 3 in the original manuscript showed both single-trial

  calibration and decoding have now been expressed as a research question. The
  motivation is further explained in Section 2.5.4 of the revised manuscript. We
  hypothesize that performance or comfort might be improved if calibration is
  performed using the VSA setting when multiple different VSA conditions are
  performed during BCI operation.
