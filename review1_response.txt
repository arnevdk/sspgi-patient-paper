  since there is to the authors' knowledge no agreed upon standardized
  classification system for BCI user capabilities within the field. Some readers
  may find the Wolpaw criteria more clear due to its simplicity. This system has
  now also been explained clearly, since it is the main system used in
  recruitment for this study according to the inclusion criterion. However,
  some readers prefer the more granular and potentially more widely used
  classification presented by Kübler and Birbaumer more helpful. Both systems

  are included, with Wolpaw's classification as the main system and the
  Kübler-Birbaumer classification as reference for interested readers or those
  who are more familiar with this classification. The table's description now
  clarifies this.

Section 2.2
  * We did indeed define for ourselves a semi-rigid list of questions to ask the
    participants for assessing their eye movements and vision. These questions
    are now included in appendix C. Where possible, the assessments obtained
    from these questions where completed with details from the medical file or
    an oculomotor assessment provided by the clinician.

  * The line between minor and major impairment in our earlier assessment of
    subjects was drawn rather subjectively. We have now reevaluated this using
    fixed rules: Major impairment implies that the impairment was complete,
    or the ability on its own prevents or nearly prevents the participant from
    operating a regular visual oddball BCI as a healthy control subject would,
    after potential corrections.
    Minor impairment implies the ability is limited to discomfort or does not on
    its own strictly prevent the user from operating a visual BCI as a healthy
    control subject would, after potential corrections. These rules have now
    been included in the methods section on line TODO. Following these rules,
    involuntary movements of participants B1 and C3 were reclassified from
    minor to major.

  * The structured questionnaire used for our assessment included age,
    handedness, sex, exact neuromuscular diagnosis, time of neuromuscular
    diagnosis, stage of neuromuscular disease if applicable, and ophthalmolocigal
    diagnosis. These were answered by the participant or the paramedical team.
    Further details concerning communication, pragmatics and presentation of the
    participant  were observed by the experimenters or through conversation
    with the participant or the paramedical team. Other relevant clinical
    details were obtained from the clinician where possible. This is now clearly
    stated at the end of section 2.1 and appendix B has been added to provide
    the questionnaire.

  * All Participants wore their corrective lenses during the assessment of eye
    movements and visual acuity, and during the BCI session. This is clarified
    in the revised manuscript on line TODO.
